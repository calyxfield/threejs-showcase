Assemble a blueprint

Choose a part. Move its ghost onto a visible connector and click to place. Green is clear; red explains why placement is blocked.

Q / E rotate around the attachment. C chooses another connector on the held part. Space resets rotation.

Click a placed part to lift it and its children. Esc cancels the move. Delete removes the held subtree; Ctrl Z restores it.

1 / 2 / 3 change view. Scroll to zoom; right-drag to orbit. The tinted boxes show conservative part clearance.

Flight: M switches to the orbit map. Drag to orbit Aster, scroll to zoom, and [ / ] select any actual ship or separated body. The map predicts unpowered coasting from current position and velocity; Click the selected orbit to place a maneuver. Edit its prograde, radial and normal components, then commit. Enter warps toward the earliest ship’s preparation point, 60 seconds before ignition. Point and use Z/X or Shift/Ctrl to burn manually. P pauses. Warp requires all throttles cut and settled powered rotation; free spin continues with wheels and RCS disabled. Ship corridors and the surface stop warp. W/S, A/D, Q/E steer; Shift/Ctrl adjust throttle; Z full, X cutoff; Space fires the next engine stage; T damping; CapsLock precision; [ / ] select a ship; B returns to its blueprint. The core contains wheels and a finite battery. Engines opens per-block enable switches. Axial separators release the entire attached subtree. Add their release icon to a stage; Spring push optionally supplies 0.2 m/s relative separation. Engines, fuel and core hardware stay with their parts. R toggles installed RCS; I/K, J/L and H/N translate; CapsLock uses 15% command precision. RCS blocks consume the selected body’s own tank propellant. Their placement determines force and torque. Compatible docking faces attract at short range and join at physical contact. Click a visible joined port to undock, then back clear before reapproach. Joined engines keep their own section’s fuel; joined-craft planning and warp are unavailable until release. There is no landing gear. Planet contact pauses the test. Ship pairs contact their conservative part boxes with bounded, inelastic frictional impulses; very fast motion or deep overlap pauses this prototype. Yard collisions are not simulated. Both separated bodies remain visible and selectable. Checkpoints restore the complete experimental world.
